The Technology

Same technology as Tobii.
Open source. $149.

NeuroGaze uses Pupil-Corneal Reflection (P-CR) tracking — the same fundamental method as every professional eye tracker — using commodity IR components that cost $53 in parts.

1
IR LED Ring
8 near-infrared LEDs illuminate the eye invisibly. The cornea reflects a precise bright reference point (Purkinje image).
2
IR Camera
A modified NoIR camera captures the IR reflection at 60fps. The pupil appears pitch black in IR — high contrast in any lighting.
3
P-CR Vector
The vector from corneal reflection to pupil center is computed. This vector changes with gaze but NOT head movement — head-independent tracking.
4
NeuroSketch App
Calibrated gaze coordinates drive the cursor. Dwell to click, blink for alternate input, scanning mode for minimal control.

Who this helps

Six ways NeuroGaze
changes lives.

🧠
ALS Communication
People with ALS lose speech and movement progressively. Eye tracking restores their ability to communicate, write, and control their environment as paralysis advances.
✍
Quadriplegia Independence
Quadriplegic users control computers, smart home devices, and AAC apps entirely through eye movement. NeuroGaze makes this accessible without insurance or $15K devices.
🎯
Eye Rehabilitation
Our gamified rehab mode tracks eye movement accuracy over time, providing measurable progress data for stroke and TBI patients recovering motor control.
📉
Research Platform
University labs pay $2,000–25,000 per eye tracking setup. NeuroGaze provides the same capability at $149 with a free Python API for data collection.
🌐
Global Access
Works offline. No subscription. Ships as open hardware plans anyone can build locally. For the first time, a paralyzed child in Lagos has the same tools as one in Boston.
🎮
AR/VR Development
Developers building foveated rendering, gaze-based UI, and eye-controlled games can prototype with NeuroGaze at a fraction of the cost of dev kits.
